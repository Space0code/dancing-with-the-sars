    How to read this

    What finds the changes

    Radar satellites (NASA and India's NISAR, and Europe's Sentinel-1) send out their own signal and measure the echo that comes back. So they see the ground through clouds and at night. When trees are cut, a building goes up or earth is moved, the echo changes, and that is what the app looks for.

    What the colors mean

    • Expected. The change matches something on record, like a permitted building in a planned area, a recorded wildfire or routine farming.
    • Needs review. Nothing on record explains it, or it is on land where building is not allowed. Worth a look.
    • High concern. A large change inside a protected area, or on land where building is not allowed with nothing on record. Look here first.
    • Not judged yet. The radar found the change, but it has not been checked against public records yet.

    The change card

    Click a colored shape to open its card: what changed, when, how big, the color it got and every reason behind it with its source. Below that are pictures from before and after, and a chart of the radar signal over time.

    The timeline and the photos

    The slider shows every change the radar had seen by that date. Press play to watch them pile up. In photo view the background is a photo from that year (aerial photos in Catalonia, satellite photos elsewhere), so you can see the land itself. The photos are there for context; the changes come from the radar.

    What it can't do

    • The satellites take a new look every few days, not every day.
    • The app points out changes for a person to check. It does not decide whether anyone broke a rule, or who is responsible.
    • False alarms happen: rain, snow, harvests and plowing also change the echo. Always look at the evidence.
    • Outlines are approximate, and very small changes can be missed.
