    How to read this

    What you're looking at

    Each coloured shape is a place where the ground changed between satellite passes. The big picture under the shapes follows the timeline: Radar shows the radar picture of that date, Satellite the latest cloud-free colour photo up to that date. Press play to watch the land change and the shapes appear in the order they were seen. Compare before and after puts the first picture and the picture of the slider date side by side: drag the line.

    How radar sees

    Radar sends its own signal and measures the echo, so it works at night and through clouds. Trees, bare soil and buildings echo differently, so a change in the echo means a change on the ground.

    The three labels

    • Expected: the change matches public records (a planned project, a registered building, a recorded wildfire).
    • Needs review: no record we could check explains the change, or the land is not zoned for building.
    • High concern: the change is inside a protected area, or it is large and no record explains it.
    • Not judged yet: grey shapes have not been checked against records yet.

    What a card shows

    What changed, when and how big; the label and the reasons behind it, with their sources; and the radar signal over time.

    Limits

    • A satellite passes every few days, not every day.
    • A label is a prompt for a person to check, not a legal judgment.
    • Rain, harvests, snow and fire can look like change, so each card says how sure the detection is.
    • Only public records that a computer can read are checked; permits kept as documents are not.

    Data

    NISAR radar (NASA and ISRO) and Sentinel-1 radar (through NASA's OPERA products); public records from the Catalan and Spanish governments. Colour photos: Landsat and Sentinel-2 (NASA HLS). Background outside the picture: Esri World Imagery.
